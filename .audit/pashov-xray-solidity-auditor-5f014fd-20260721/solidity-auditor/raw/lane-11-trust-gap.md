# Lane 11 — Trust-Gap Hunter

[Feynman: RetentionReceiver.claimEmissions] This function counts how many protocol weeks have passed since the last claim, collects all accumulated emissions, asks the treasury for a weekly amount multiplied by that count, sends as much of the combined balance as remains under the lifetime retention cap, and returns any excess to the treasury. The fuzzy point is that it prices every elapsed week using the one rate stored at claim time, even though governance can change that rate after some or all of those weeks have elapsed.

[Feynman: RetentionReceiver.setTreasuryAllocationPerEpoch] This function replaces the weekly treasury contribution immediately. It does not first settle weeks accrued at the old contribution and does not record the epoch from which the new contribution begins.

[Socratic: src/dao/emissions/receivers/RetentionReceiver.sol:70 — why?] Why may the current `treasuryAllocationPerEpoch` price all historical `epochsSince` rather than only epochs after the setter call? The implicit belief is that the rate never changes while claims are outstanding, or that retroactive repricing is intended; neither condition is enforced.

[Inversion: RetentionReceiver.claimEmissions] (1) Let four epochs accrue at 34,255 RSUP, raise the rate to 50,000 RSUP, then call the permissionless claim to charge all four at 50,000; (2) let epochs accrue at a high rate, lower it immediately before claiming, and erase the beneficiaries' accrued treasury allocation; (3) perform the repricing while `distributedRewards` is just below `MAX_REWARDS` so the retroactive amount consumes the remaining lifetime program budget earlier than the old schedule allowed.

path: Core governance changes `treasuryAllocationPerEpoch` without settling accrued epochs → any caller invokes `claimEmissions` → `epochsSince` historical epochs are multiplied by the new rate → retention beneficiaries gain or lose treasury-funded RSUP relative to the rate that applied while those epochs elapsed.
seam: access×economics×asymmetry
actor: A retention beneficiary (or any account acting for that beneficiary class) can permissionlessly trigger the claim immediately after the Core-authorized rate change; treasury and retention beneficiaries are the asymmetrically affected classes.

Core then calls `setTreasuryAllocationPerEpoch(50_000e18)`. `claimEmissions` computes `epochsSince = 104 - 100 = 4` at lines 56-60 and then pulls `50_000e18 * 4 = 200_000e18` at lines 68-71, retroactively moving `62_980e18` more from treasury into retention rewards. Reversing the rate change retroactively removes the same delta from beneficiaries. The `MAX_REWARDS` check only caps lifetime distribution; it does not preserve the rate applicable to each elapsed epoch.
seam_proof: The setter's Core-only guard is correct, the multiplication in `claimEmissions` is arithmetically correct, and permissionless claiming is independently safe, but the privileged write and the catch-up formula combine to let one user class reprice another class's already accrued value.
root_defect: A mutable per-epoch rate is applied to an uncheckpointed interval instead of being versioned or settling the interval before the rate changes.
description: Changing the treasury allocation rate retroactively reprices every unclaimed epoch, shifting RSUP between treasury and retention beneficiaries and potentially exhausting the lifetime cap early.
fix: Before changing the rate, settle or checkpoint all elapsed epochs at the old rate (or store epoch-versioned rates), and make the new value effective only from the checkpointed/current epoch onward.

[Feynman: RedemptionOperator.executeRedemption] An approved bot chooses a market, a loan size, the least reUSD it will accept, the least profit it will accept, and the largest redemption fee it will tolerate; the contract then borrows, trades through fixed venues, redeems reUSD for collateral, repays the loan, and sends whatever loan asset remains to treasury. The fuzzy point is that the bot which can profit from adverse ordering also chooses every economic safety bound protecting the treasury.

[Feynman: RedemptionOperator._handleFlashCallback] This function authenticates the lender and loan amount, performs several trades, redeems the resulting reUSD, checks that the contract's entire ending loan-asset balance covers repayment plus the bot-selected profit, repays the lender, and gives the entire remainder to treasury. It does not separate pre-existing balance from proceeds of this attempt.

[Socratic: src/dao/operators/RedemptionOperator.sol:362 — why?] Why is a caller-supplied `minProfit` checked against the contract's full balance rather than a protocol-set profit floor measured from the pre-call balance? The implicit belief is that approved callers share treasury's economic incentives and that the operator never holds loan-asset value before a call.

[Inversion: RedemptionOperator.executeRedemption] (1) Supply `minProfit = 0` and sandwich the fixed Curve route until the ending balance is only one wei above repayment; (2) supply `minReusdFromSwap = 0` and `maxFeePct = type(uint256).max` so neither the swap nor redemption fee protects treasury's expected margin; (3) exploit the zero-minimum intermediate swaps and full-balance check to make a losing round trip consume CRVUSD/FRXUSD already sitting on the operator while the sandwich recovers that value externally.

[Feynman: ResupplyRegistry.setStaker] This governance function tells the rest of the system which staking contract is current. It is the switch that enables permanent-stake migration and directs callers which staker to use next.

[Feynman: RewardHandler.constructor] At deployment, this function looks up the then-current staker once, saves it forever, and approves that address to pull revenue tokens.

[Feynman: RewardHandler.queueStakingRewards] The authorized weekly fee operator calls this function to send all revenue waiting on the handler into the staker saved at handler deployment, then distributes governance-token emissions elsewhere. It never checks whether the registry now names a different staker.

[Socratic: src/protocol/RewardHandler.sol:63 — why?] Why is the registry-selected staker cached immutably when `ResupplyRegistry.setStaker` and `GovStaker.migrateStake` explicitly make that dependency replaceable? The implicit belief is that every staker switch is accompanied by a perfectly synchronized RewardHandler replacement, but no on-chain transition enforces it.

[Inversion: RewardHandler.queueStakingRewards] (1) Stay in the old staker while most holders migrate, then collect the old handler's next revenue stream with much larger relative weight; (2) trigger the authorized weekly fee distribution in the interval after `setStaker` but before a replacement handler is active; (3) migrate to the new staker and show that new stake receives zero revenue while even a tiny old-staker holdout receives all revenue still routed through the immutable address.

[Feynman: GovStaker.migrateStake] A permanently locked account may move its realized tokens from an old staker to the registry's new staker once governance disables the old cooldown; after depositing the tokens, the old staker calls a hook that is supposed to preserve the account's permanent status.

[Feynman: GovStaker.onPermaStakeMigrate] In the production base contract this externally callable migration hook does nothing, so by itself it neither authenticates the old staker nor marks the account permanent.

[Socratic: src/dao/staking/GovStaker.sol:432 — why?] Why does the only cross-contract step intended to carry an irreversible status have an empty production implementation? The implicit belief is that every successor will override it correctly, but the deployable base and registry setter do not enforce that property.

[Inversion: GovStaker.migrateStake] (1) Configure the deployable base `GovStaker` as successor and migrate a permanent account, leaving its new `isPermaStaker` false; (2) after its pending stake realizes, call `cooldown` and later `unstake` to recover supposedly permanent RSUP; (3) use `PermaStaker.execute` as its owner/operator to issue those cooldown and unstake calls from the wrapper account itself.
